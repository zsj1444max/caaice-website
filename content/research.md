## What we are working on

CAAICE is new. Rather than list publications we have not yet written, here are the questions we are actually working on. Our first outputs will address them directly.

### How will Southeast Asia's data centres be powered?

Malaysian data centre electricity demand is projected to grow roughly sevenfold by 2030. The Johor state government has already rejected a substantial share of applications, citing energy efficiency among its reasons. We are examining what power procurement options actually exist for these facilities, what the rules permit, and where the binding constraints sit.

### Who pays for the grid?

New industrial load of this scale requires transmission investment. The question of how those costs are allocated between data centre operators, other industrial users and households is one of the most consequential — and least examined — questions in Asian energy policy today.

### Can clean power cross borders fast enough?

Regional interconnection has been discussed for decades and delivered slowly. We are studying the legal and regulatory mechanisms for cross-border renewable trade, and why they underperform relative to the political commitment behind them.

### How is climate litigation reshaping project risk?

In 2024 South Korea's Constitutional Court found parts of the country's climate legislation unconstitutional for failing to protect future generations. Cases across the region are testing similar arguments. We track how this emerging body of law changes the risk landscape for energy and data infrastructure projects.

## How we work with data

Where we can, we publish our underlying data and state our assumptions plainly. Where a number is uncertain, we say so. Where we are reasoning rather than measuring, we label it.

We would rather publish a narrower claim we can defend than a broader one we cannot.
